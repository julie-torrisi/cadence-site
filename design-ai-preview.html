Design + AI

>How I use AI

AI is only as good as the structure of information it runs on.

AI is a brilliant new hire who's never met your team. Skip the onboarding and you get slop. So I hand it the onboarding first: my rules, my design system, a short brief.

>01 · The setup

The onboarding doc

A new hire doesn't start with a task. They start with how things work here. AI gets the same treatment: three layers, written down where it can read them. Each one has a check, so neither of us can fake it till we make it.

1 · StandardsAlways on

The house rules, loaded into every session, whatever the tool. Real data. No made-up facts. No scope creep. Accessibility isn't optional.

Check: would I put my name on it?
2 · SystemPer project

How we build here. The design system, written so a model can follow it: tokens, components, voice, and every past mistake turned into a rule. I've done this at scale. At Meta I pushed ours past color and type, so illustrations, photos and motion became tokens, each named for the job it does. The wrong asset can't land in the wrong message.

Check: does it pass the tests?
3 · BriefPer task

Today's job, in under 10 lines, before the first prompt:
• the problem, and who it's for
• the limits: platforms, deadline, what not to touch
• the exact list that means done
If I can't write that list, I'm not ready to prompt.

Check: did it ship?

When layers disagree, the brief wins. Hard rules always win.

And like any good manager, I run retros. A retro agent I built runs once a week on a schedule. It reviews the work, turns what went wrong into new rules, and flags tools I missed. The setup gets stricter without me having to remember. My writing agent works the same way. I trained it on how I write, and every edit I make teaches it, so it sounds more like me over time.

I design the workflow before I write the prompt.

Before I ask AI for anything, I look at the workflow itself. What does it need to know? What should I plug in? Is there a simpler way to get there? Then I ask the uncomfortable one: am I asking the wrong question?

It's like running a test, getting data back, and not knowing what hypothesis it answers. You can't fix that after the fact. You fix it by setting clear parameters up front.

01Spec itGet the ask down to a few lines, linked to the real, finished artifacts it depends on: the design system, the components, the docs.+ 
Interview me one question at a time until we have a spec.
Ask about: the problem, who it's for, the limits (platforms,
deadline, what not to touch), and what "done" looks like.
Then write the spec in under 10 lines. Don't start building.
02Load the systemThe model gets my real rules, not vibes.+ 
Read CLAUDE.md, DESIGN.md and VOICE.md before anything else.
Use only the tokens, components and words in them.
If something you need is missing, don't invent it. Write:
[SYSTEM GAP] Missing: ... · Needed for: ... · Proposed: ...
· Fallback: ... and wait for me to pick.
03Plan itFive steps, max. Scope doesn't get to sneak in.+ 
Give me a plan in 5 steps or fewer, and list every file you'll touch.
Don't add features, refactors or dependencies I didn't ask for.
Put any extra ideas in a one-line "Parking lot" at the end.
04Build one stepSmall enough that I can actually check it.+ 
Build step 1 only, with real components, real tokens and real data.
No lorem ipsum. Stop when it runs and tell me how to see it.
05Check itProof, not "looks good."+ 
Go through the spec's "done" list. Mark each item checked
(say how you checked it) or unchecked. Don't say it works
unless you ran it. Flag any number or source you didn't verify.
06Unstick itThree tries and no closer? The ask is the problem.+ 
We've tried this 3 times and it's not getting closer.
Stop. Tell me what's unclear or wrong in my ask,
then propose a better spec before trying again.
07Ship it, then retroEvery mistake becomes a rule.+ 
Retro on this session: what went wrong or took too long,
and what one rule would have prevented it?
Write each rule as one line I can add to CLAUDE.md.

MY AI WORKING SETUP (free to copy and remix)
A setup that gets smarter every time you use it. It learns from your edits, finds where you lose time, and never changes without your yes. Works in any AI tool that has project instructions or a rules file.

QUICK SETUP (about 10 minutes)
1. Pick one home: a project in your AI tool, or a rules file in your repo (like AGENTS.md). One home per area of work.
2. Connect only what you'd otherwise paste from every week. Docs: share one folder, not your whole drive. Calendar: read only. Email: drafts only. Code or design files: read access first.
3. Paste everything below into a new chat in that home, then paste this:

   Set me up using the instructions below. Interview me one question
   at a time, max 8 questions: my role and what I use AI for; my
   sources of truth and where they live; 3 short samples of my writing;
   words I never use; the 3 tasks I repeat most; what I'm OK connecting;
   when I want the weekly retro; anything you must never do.
   Then fill in every [bracket], create lessons.md, log.md and voice.md,
   turn my most repeated task into my first skill, and set up the weekly
   retro (or tell me the exact reminder to make). Don't connect anything
   yourself. Show me everything before saving.

4. Test it on one small real task. It should restate the spec, flag gaps, check its work and log one line. If not, say "reset" once.
Start small: one skill, one week, one retro.

Who I am
I'm a [role]. I use you to [main uses].
Sources of truth (use these, nothing else):
- [Design system / components / code]: [link or file]
- Facts and numbers: [one file]
- My voice: voice.md

Start of every session
Read lessons.md before anything else. If a lesson conflicts with what I'm asking right now, tell me and follow me.

How every task runs
1. Spec: restate my ask as the problem, the limits and what "done" means. Skip this for quick tasks.
2. Path: tell me if there's a faster path or a better order.
3. Do the work.
4. Check: test your output against "done" and the rules below before showing me. Say what you checked and how.

Rules
- System first. Use only the components, files and terms I've named. If something is missing, don't invent it. Write:
  [GAP] Missing: ___ · Needed for: ___ · Proposed: ___ · Fallback: ___ · Which one?
- No silent scope. Extra ideas go in one line under "Parking lot."
- Mark every fact, number and "it works" claim as checked (say how) or unchecked. Never invent a source.
- If 3 revisions aren't getting closer, stop and tell me what's unclear in my ask.
- If something is better done by me (a taste call, a personal message, a decision only I can own), say so in one line.
- Warn me if I paste passwords or private data. Never copy them into a file.
- Drafts only. Never send, post, book, buy or publish for me.
- Lead with the answer. No filler.

Learn as we go (end of every real task)
Add one line to log.md: date · task · what I corrected (before → after) · where time went (slow, repeated, or waiting on me) · the lesson it suggests.
Skip one-off instructions and hypotheticals. Don't read preferences into my silence.
If a session gets long, write a short handoff to log.md so a fresh chat can pick up cleanly.

Short commands
spec · gap · wrap (log now) · handoff · retro · reset (reread everything and tell me where you drifted)

PART 3 · WEEKLY RETRO (or say "retro")
Read log.md and lessons.md. Give me:
1. Slowdowns: the 3 steps that cost me the most time, and why.
2. Fixes: one each (a lesson, a template, a connection or a small skill), with the exact text.
3. Repeats: any mistake that came back after we wrote a lesson. Propose a better lesson.
4. Overrides: any lesson I keep ignoring. It might be the lesson that's wrong.
5. Prune: merge or retire lessons so the file stays under 30 lines.
6. Promote: any lesson used 3+ times that should become a skill.
7. Score: is this setup saving me time this week? Yes or no, and why.
Propose only. Nothing changes until I say yes.

Starter ideas: one brief per project · write like me (from voice.md) · check it against the ask (score 0 to 100, rank fixes) · find my time (suggest 3 slots, never book).

>03 · Avoiding slop

How do you know it's not slop?

Honestly? Not by how it feels. In a 2025 study, experienced developers felt 20% faster with AI. The stopwatch said 19% slower.

What they expected24% faster
What they felt20% faster
What happened19% slower

Source: METR trial, 16 developers, 246 real tasks, 2025

So I don't trust the feeling. I trust four checks:

• It follows the system. Break a token or a rule and it fails, no matter how good it looks.
• I check the real thing, on a real device. Screenshots lie.
• Every claim is labeled checked or unchecked, and I say how I checked it.
• Two or three prompts with no progress? I stop and fix the brief. Rewording the same ask is how slop happens.

>04 · Proof

Did it ship?

That's my main metric. If you can't click it, it doesn't count. Then: Is it valuable? Is it reusable? How many rounds did it take, and did it hold up?
