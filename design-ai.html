> 01 · The setup

Three layers, every prompt

I don't just chat with AI. Every prompt sits on three layers, and each one has a check so I can't fool myself.

1 · StandardsAlways on

My standing rules, loaded into every session in Claude, Claude Code and Cursor: real data, no made-up facts, no scope creep, and accessibility is not optional.

Check: would I put my name on it?
2 · SystemPer project

The design system, written so a model can follow it: tokens, components, voice, and every past mistake turned into a rule.

Check: does it pass the tests?
3 · BriefPer task

A short spec before the first prompt: the problem, the limits, and what done looks like.

Check: did it ship?

If two layers disagree, the brief wins. Hard rules always win.

The part that keeps it honest: after each session, a retro agent I built pulls out what went wrong and turns it into new rules. The system gets stricter without me remembering to make it stricter.

My workflow, with the prompts I actually use

01Spec itBefore any tool, I get the ask down to a few lines.+ 
Interview me one question at a time until we have a spec.
Ask about: the problem, who it's for, the limits (platforms,
deadline, what not to touch), and what "done" looks like.
Then write the spec in under 10 lines. Don't start building.
02Load the systemThe model gets my real rules, not vibes.+ 
Read CLAUDE.md, DESIGN.md and VOICE.md before anything else.
Use only the tokens, components and words in them.
If something you need is missing, don't invent it. Write:
[SYSTEM GAP] Missing: ... · Needed for: ... · Proposed: ...
· Fallback: ... and wait for me to pick.
03Plan itA short plan first, so scope can’t sneak in.+ 
Give me a plan in 5 steps or fewer, and list every file you'll touch.
Don't add features, refactors or dependencies I didn't ask for.
Put any extra ideas in a one-line "Parking lot" at the end.
04Build one stepSmall steps I can actually check.+ 
Build step 1 only, with real components, real tokens and real data.
No lorem ipsum. Stop when it runs and tell me how to see it.
05Check itProof, not "looks good."+ 
Go through the spec's "done" list. Mark each item checked
(say how you checked it) or unchecked. Don't say it works
unless you ran it. Flag any number or source you didn't verify.
06Unstick itIf it’s not converging, the ask is the problem.+ 
We've tried this 3 times and it's not getting closer.
Stop. Tell me what's unclear or wrong in my ask,
then propose a better spec before trying again.
07Ship it, then retroShip it, then turn mistakes into rules.+ 
Retro on this session: what went wrong or took too long,
and what one rule would have prevented it?
Write each rule as one line I can add to CLAUDE.md.

> 03 · Avoiding slop

Feeling fast isn't being fast

In a 2025 study, experienced developers said AI made them faster. A stopwatch said otherwise.

What they expected24% faster
What they felt20% faster
What happened19% slower

Source: METR trial, 16 developers, 246 real tasks, 2025

So I don't trust the feeling. I write the spec first, check the real thing on a real device, and if two or three prompts don't get me closer, I stop and fix the spec instead of rewording.

> 04 · Proof

Did it ship?

That's the main metric, along with how many rounds it took and whether it held up. If you can't click it, it doesn't count.
